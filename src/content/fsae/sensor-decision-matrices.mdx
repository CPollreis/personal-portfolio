Every sensor on the car earns its place by winning a small argument: what it
measures, how well, at what compute and money cost, and whether anything cheaper
already covers it. These are the rough cuts, one matrix per domain. Numbers are
order-of-magnitude and get replaced with datasheet values and measured compute
load as the stack comes up.

## Perception: seeing the cones

The primary job is detecting blue, yellow, and orange cones out to the braking
distance at target speed, in daylight, on a dusty track.

| Option | Usable range | 3D accuracy | Colour / class | Compute load | Dust & sun | Rel. cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spinning 3D LiDAR | 50-120 m | High | None (needs fusion) | Medium (point cloud) | Good | High |
| Solid-state LiDAR | 30-90 m | High | None | Medium | Good, narrow FOV | Medium |
| Stereo camera | 10-40 m | Medium, degrades with range | Native | High (disparity + CNN) | Sun flare, needs exposure control | Low |
| Mono camera + CNN | 10-50 m (learned depth) | Low, scale ambiguous | Native | Medium (CNN only) | Sun flare | Low |
| Automotive radar | 5-150 m | Low lateral | None | Low | Excellent | Medium |

Rough lean: **LiDAR for geometry, camera for colour, fused**. LiDAR gives
range-accurate cone centroids the motion planner can trust; the camera projects
colour onto those detections. Radar stays a maybe for a weather and
high-closing-speed backup, not a primary.

## Localisation and state estimation

Where the car is on the track, and how fast it is changing, feeding the SLAM and
control loops.

| Option | Fix type | Drift | Rate | Depends on | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wheel encoders / odometry | Relative | Grows with distance, slip-sensitive | High (1 kHz) | Traction | Short-term velocity |
| MEMS IMU (6/9-axis) | Relative | Seconds to divergence | High (1 kHz) | Nothing | Attitude, accel, gyro |
| Single GNSS | Absolute | Bounded, 1-3 m | Low (10 Hz) | Sky view | Coarse global fix |
| RTK-GNSS | Absolute | Bounded, 2-5 cm | Low-medium (10-20 Hz) | Base station / correction link | Ground truth, map anchor |
| Visual-inertial odometry | Relative | Slow, loop-closure correctable | Medium (30-60 Hz) | Texture, lighting | Pose between GNSS fixes |
| LiDAR odometry / SLAM | Relative + map | Low with cone map loop closure | Medium (10-20 Hz) | Cone density | Primary track-frame pose |

Rough lean: **IMU + wheel odometry fused in an EKF for the fast state, LiDAR
cone-SLAM for the track-frame pose, RTK-GNSS as the map anchor and sanity
check**. No single sensor is both absolute and drift-free at rate.

## Vehicle dynamics and chassis

Sensors the driverless stack needs from the car itself, most of which a
well-instrumented manual car already has.

| Sensor | Measures | Why the stack needs it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steering angle sensor | Rack position / road-wheel angle | Closes the steering loop, feeds the bicycle model |
| Wheel speed sensors (x4) | Per-corner rotational speed | Velocity estimate, slip ratio, launch and braking control |
| Linear suspension pots | Damper displacement | Ride height, load transfer, rough surface detection |
| Brake pressure transducers | Front / rear circuit pressure | Confirms braking commands, part of the EBS proof |
| Steering torque / current | Motor effort at the column | Fault detection, end-stop and jam sensing |
| Load cells / strain gauges | Pushrod or upright load | Optional: tyre load model, validation only |

Rough lean: **steering angle, four wheel speeds, and brake pressure are
mandatory** for a driverless entry; suspension pots are high value and cheap;
load cells are a nice-to-have for offline model validation.

## Powertrain and HV pack

Electric-car sensing that the autonomous system reads but does not own.

| Quantity | Options | Trade-off | Pick |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pack current | Hall-effect vs shunt + isolated amp | Hall: isolated, drifts with temp. Shunt: accurate, needs isolation | Hall for main, shunt for calibration |
| Cell voltage | Direct taps into the BMS AFE | Wiring count vs resolution | Per-cell, no compromise |
| Pack temperature | NTC thermistor vs thermocouple vs RTD | NTC: cheap, non-linear, enough. TC: wide range, needs cold junction. RTD: linear, pricey | NTC at the cells, spot TCs on busbars |
| Motor position | Resolver vs hall vs optical encoder | Resolver: robust, analog front end. Hall: cheap, coarse. Encoder: precise, fragile in heat and vibration | Resolver, per the inverter |
| DC-link voltage | Isolated divider into the inverter | Accuracy vs isolation rating | Inverter-integrated |
| Insulation resistance | Insulation monitoring device (IMD) | Rules-mandated, not optional | Bender or equivalent |

## Safety and autonomous-specific

- **EBS pressure sensors** on both brake circuits, redundant, to prove the
  emergency brake actually built pressure.
- **ASMS and RES state** wired as hard digital inputs to the autonomous
  controller, not read over CAN, so a dead bus cannot mask a stop request.
- **Redundancy rule of thumb:** anything that can command motion or stop it
  gets two independent sensing paths that disagree loudly.
