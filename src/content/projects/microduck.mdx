## What it is

**Microduck** is a 25 cm, sub-800 g biped from Pollen Robotics: 15 servos,
a camera, an 8x8 ToF depth sensor, two IMUs, and a grasping beak, all driven
by learned policies, neural nets trained in simulation and run on-device at
50 Hz rather than hand-tuned gaits. It walks, crouches, recovers from a fall,
and can even roller-skate. Pollen open-sourced the entire stack: sim
environments, training code, and the trained policies themselves.

Hugging Face acquired Pollen Robotics in 2025, and on September 2, 2026,
Nvidia signed a definitive agreement to acquire Hugging Face for $12.9B,
which makes Microduck, oddly, an Nvidia robot in waiting even though the
board itself runs a Rockchip RK3566, not Jetson silicon. Pre-orders opened
August 27, 2026 and reportedly cleared 10,000 units almost immediately.
Mine is in that backlog.

## The plan

Nothing has arrived yet, so this entry is the plan, not results:

- **Start in sim.** The community has already ported Microduck's tasks
  (locomotion, ball-kick, two-robot rally) to **Isaac Lab 3.0** on the newer
  Newton/MJWarp physics backend. That's the fastest path to a working policy
  before the hardware even ships, and it's the same RL loop (PPO over a
  physics sim, sim-to-real transfer) as the [Rocket League
  bot](/projects/rocket-league-rl-bot), just on a real robot instead of a
  headless game engine.
- **Sim-to-real.** Once the robot lands, the goal is deploying a policy
  trained purely in sim straight onto the hardware, and seeing how much the
  gap actually costs.
- **Move up the Nvidia stack.** Longer term, since Microduck is about to sit
  inside Nvidia's robotics org, the plan is to follow it there: rebuild the
  training environment on Nvidia's own **Isaac Sim** and eventually
  **Isaac GR00T** tooling as that ecosystem absorbs Hugging Face's robots,
  rather than staying on the community Isaac Lab port indefinitely.

</Step>
