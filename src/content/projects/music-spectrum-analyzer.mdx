I wanted to actually *see* music, physically, on the wall, so I'm building a
**32-band spectrum analyzer**: an acrylic panel of LED columns that turns live
audio into a real-time frequency display, sitting next to my actual sound
system rather than replacing it. It's a work in progress, and it's as much a
signal-routing problem as a DSP one.

<Step>

## Signal path

The core is a **Teensy 4.1** doing the FFT in software. I picked it over an
ESP32 specifically because I ended up needing two input paths, wired S/PDIF
from a PC and Bluetooth from a phone, and no single chip does both well:
Bluetooth Classic (A2DP) only exists on the original ESP32-WROOM-32, while USB
Audio Class only exists on the Teensy and ESP32-S2/S3. Rather than fight one
chip into doing both, wired audio became the Teensy's native job and Bluetooth
got bolted on as an external receiver module instead of a second
microcontroller.

The default 1024-point FFT gives 43Hz per bin at 44.1kHz, which makes the
bottom few bands look mushy and correlated, so I'm running a 4096-point
transform with CMSIS-DSP instead, around 10.8Hz per bin, using the Teensy
4.1's hardware FPU. The 640 **WS2812** LEDs are arranged as a 32-by-20 matrix
and driven with **OctoWS2811**, which splits the strip across 8 parallel
outputs on FlexIO and DMA so frame rate isn't capped by pushing 640 pixels
down one wire.

</Step>

## Getting audio in

Two sources need to reach the analyzer, my PC over USB and my phone over
Bluetooth, without either one touching the actual listening path.

The PC connects over USB-C to a **FiiO K11 R2R** DAC I already own. Its line
out feeds my **Denon UDRA-M7** amp and **Klipsch R-41** speakers exactly like
normal, and because the K11's coax jack outputs a live S/PDIF copy whenever
it's in USB mode, that same connector doubles as a free digital tap for the
analyzer.

The phone connects over Bluetooth to a **1Mii B06HD+** receiver, which puts
out optical and coax S/PDIF at the same time, the one spec worth actually
confirming before buying, since most cheap receivers switch between outputs
rather than running both at once. Optical goes into the K11's optical input
so Bluetooth plays over the same DAC and amp; coax goes down to the analyzer
as its second candidate feed.

The Teensy only has one S/PDIF input, so a **toggle switch** on the front-end
board picks between the two coax sources before either signal gets processed,
rather than building a receiver circuit for each and muxing the clean logic
afterward. On the selected line: a 75-ohm termination and a DC-blocking cap
knock the ~0.5Vpp coax signal down to something usable, then a **74LVC1GU04**
inverter, wired with a 100k-ohm feedback resistor so it biases into its linear
region, acts as a comparator and squares that into clean 3.3V logic for the
Teensy's S/PDIF input on pin 15.

The analyzer is a tap, not a link in the chain. It never sits between a
source and the speakers, so unplugging it, or it crashing, doesn't touch
playback either way. The one thing it doesn't get for free: the tap point is
upstream of both the K11's and the Denon's volume knobs, so bar height
doesn't track volume yet.

</Step>

## Power and wiring

640 WS2812s pull close to 38A at full white, though a spectrum analyzer
never gets near that. I sized the supply for the worst case anyway with a
**Mean Well LRS-150-5** (5V, 30A), fused on the output. Power goes straight to
the LED strips rather than through the Teensy, injected at both ends of each
80-LED run with a 1000uF capacitor at every injection point, since voltage
sag along a run is what makes the far end drift orange. Teensy, OctoWS2811,
and the power supply all share one ground point, and the Teensy's VIN-VUSB
pads get cut since it's powered off the LED rail rather than USB, so it can't
backfeed into my PC.

</Step>

## CAD and mechanical design

The acrylic enclosure and mounting hardware are modeled in **SolidWorks**,
including the individual rows that hold each LED column. I wanted to build
this project for basic CAD/mech skills and to have a cool visual alongside my
speakers!

## Sourcing the acrylic

The panels are full 48"x96" cast acrylic sheets, big enough that picking them
up meant renting a **U-Haul** rather than fitting them in my car. The
mechanical side of this build has been slow moving: sourcing material,
finishing the row drawings in SolidWorks, and getting everything cut is
taking longer than the electronics.

</Step>

## Status

WIP. The front-end board, the S/PDIF receiver circuit and source-select
