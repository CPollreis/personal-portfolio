## First principles derivation

### Notation

Every symbol used in this section, for reference while reading.

**Setup**

- $t$: one time step in an episode; $T$ is the final step
- $s_t$, $a_t$, $r_t$: the state, action, and reward at step $t$
- $\tau$: a trajectory, meaning every state and action across one full episode
- $\gamma$: discount factor in $(0, 1)$; rewards further ahead count for less
- $G_t$: discounted return, the discounted sum of rewards from step $t$ onward

**Policy and value**

- $\pi_\theta$: the policy, the network that picks actions, with weight/parameters $\theta$
- $\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)$: probability the policy gives to action $a_t$ in state $s_t$
- $V_\phi$: the value function, or critic, with weight/parameters $\phi$
- $V_\phi(s_t)$: its estimate of the return from state $s_t$
- $A_t$: advantage, how much better than average an action was; $\hat{A}_t$ is the estimate used in training
- $\delta_t$: temporal-difference residual, the cheap one-step advantage estimate
- $\lambda$: GAE input in $[0, 1]$ that trades bias against variance
- $L^{VF}(\phi)$: value-function loss; $L^{PG}(\theta)$: policy-gradient objective

**PPO**

- $\theta_{\text{old}}$: the policy's parameters when the current batch was collected
- $\rho_t(\theta)$: importance ratio $\pi_\theta / \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}$; a value of $1$ means the policy has not moved
- $\epsilon$: clip width; keeps $\rho_t$ within $[1 - \epsilon,\ 1 + \epsilon]$
- $L^{CLIP}(\theta)$: PPO's clipped training objective
- $\mathbb{E}_t[\,\cdot\,]$: an average over sampled time steps

Reinforcement learning maps cleanly onto the physical world. An **agent** acts
inside an **environment** toward a goal, in **episodes** split into time steps.
At each step $t$ the agent observes a state $s_t$, takes an action $a_t$, and
receives a reward $r_t$ for the progress that action made. A full episode is a
**trajectory** of every state visited and every action taken:

$$
\tau = (s_0,\ a_0,\ s_1,\ a_1,\ \dots,\ s_T)
$$

The objective is to maximize the **discounted return** from each step, where
$\gamma \in (0, 1)$ makes rewards further in the future count for less, in an
exponentially decaying way:

$$
G_t = \sum_{k=0}^{T-t} \gamma^{k}\, r_{t+k}
$$

The **policy** $\pi_\theta$ is the network being trained, with weight/parameters
$\theta$. Feed it a state and it returns a probability distribution over
actions; the agent samples from that distribution to act. Learning means
nudging the probability of an action up or down by how good the action turned
out to be.

"How good" is the **advantage** $A_t$: how much better than average this
action was, compared to just picking at random from the current policy.
Estimating that needs a baseline, so a second network, the **value function**
$V_\phi$ with its own weight/parameters $\phi$, estimates the expected return from a
state, independent of what comes next. The policy is the **actor**, the value
function is the **critic**. The critic is trained by regression onto the
observed return:

$$
L^{VF}(\phi) = \big(V_\phi(s_t) - G_t\big)^2
$$

The cheapest advantage estimate is the one-step **temporal-difference
residual**:

$$
\delta_t = r_t + \gamma\, V_\phi(s_{t+1}) - V_\phi(s_t)
$$

It has low variance but is biased by the critic's error. Summing real rewards
to the end of the episode instead (the Monte Carlo estimate) is the opposite:
unbiased, but high variance because it trusts every noisy reward. **Generalized
advantage estimation** interpolates between the two with a second input
$\lambda$:

$$
\hat{A}_t = \sum_{k=0}^{T-t} (\gamma\lambda)^{k}\, \delta_{t+k}
$$

$\lambda$ near 0 leans on the critic (low variance, more bias); $\lambda$ near
1 leans on raw rewards (low bias, more variance).

The base policy-gradient objective pushes up the log-probability of an action,
weighted by its advantage:

$$
L^{PG}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_t\big[\log \pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)\, \hat{A}_t\big]
$$

To reuse one batch of experience for several epochs, **PPO** corrects for the
policy having moved since the batch was collected, using an importance ratio:

$$
\rho_t(\theta) = \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t \mid s_t)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t \mid s_t)}
$$

Left unconstrained, that ratio lets a single batch move the policy too far and
destabilize training. PPO's contribution is to **clip** it into a trust region
of width $\epsilon$ and take the pessimistic term:

$$
L^{CLIP}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_t\Big[\min\big(\rho_t\, \hat{A}_t,\ \operatorname{clip}(\rho_t,\ 1-\epsilon,\ 1+\epsilon)\, \hat{A}_t\big)\Big]
$$

Taking the minimum of the clipped and unclipped terms means one batch can only
improve the policy so much before the gradient flattens out. The full training
objective maximizes $L^{CLIP}$, minimizes the value loss, and optionally adds a
small entropy bonus to encourage exploration.

## Context and problem statement

Rocket League is car soccer, and almost all of its skill ceiling comes from
movement: boost management, aerials, wave-dashes, reading a bouncing ball.
That makes it a rich continuous-control problem rather than a game-theory
problem.

The **state** is the physics of the car and
ball, positions, velocities, orientations, and boost, and the **action** is
the controller: accel/reverse, steer, pitch, yaw, roll, jump, and boost.
The **reward** is nearly absent. A goal happens once every few thousand steps,
so an agent left with only "score a goal" almost never stumbles into any
signal at all.

The goal of this project is an **offline agent**, trained without ever
touching the live game loop, that is good enough to play a real 1v1 against
friends. The hard parts are exploration under sparse reward and the
sample cost of a real-time 3D game. Shaping rewards that help early
without capping how good the policy can eventually get is another.

## My implementation

**Environment.** RLGym wraps Rocket League as a Gym-style loop: car and ball
state in, controller actions out. For training I run against a headless
reimplementation of the game's physics rather than the real client, so
rollouts collect experience far faster than real time and many can run in
parallel.

**Networks.** The actor and critic are separate multilayer perceptrons over
the flattened state vector. The actor outputs the parameters of a distribution
over the mostly continuous action space; the critic outputs a single scalar
value estimate.

**Algorithm.** PPO with GAE. Rollout workers collect fixed-length segments,
GAE turns the rewards and value estimates into advantages $\hat{A}_t$ and
return targets, then several epochs of minibatch updates run on the clipped
objective. $\gamma$, $\lambda$, and $\epsilon$ are the main inputs.

**Reward shaping.** A weighted sum of terms: distance to the ball, velocity
toward the ball, ball velocity toward the opponent goal, boost pickup, and the
sparse goal reward. The dense terms are annealed down over training so they
guide the early policy and then get out of its way.

**Training loop.** Written from scratch in PyTorch so every reward term and
every PPO diagnostic is logged separately, and so changing the reward function
doesn't mean fighting a framework's abstractions.
